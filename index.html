🌧 Build your own rain scenario

Rain reaching the river = rain × duration × (1 − soaked up) × (1 − drained), compared with 37 years of real 48-hour rain in the Vesdre catchment to find its yearly chance; the flood map for that chance is interpolated between the EFAS maps. A simple index, not a hydrological model: short storms on small streams, snowmelt and blocked bridges are not represented.

Safety zones

Based on all flood sizes together, not only the one you picked.

Shelters

    Chosen from real schools, sports halls and community centres (overnight shelters) and stadiums and parks (assembly points only). Must be dry in every flood size, not flooded in 2021, at least 150 m from flood water. Ranked with equal weight on: at-risk residents within 1.5 km, capacity, height above flood water, distance to water; no dry road access halves the score. Capacity = floor area × floors ÷ 3.5 m² per person (Sphere minimum); type your own number to override. Check kitchens, toilets, accessibility and the building owner on site.

    Critical sites in the water

    Hospitals, clinics, fire stations, police, town halls flooded in this flood size: evacuate or protect them first.

      Evacuation plan

        Forecast: which plan?

        Rain and river flow from Open-Meteo (external source: GloFAS river model ~5 km, ERA5 / forecast models). A small river like the Vesdre is only roughly captured: check the official river gauges (SPW hydrometry) before acting.

        Emergency teams: who goes where

        Real fire stations, police stations and hospitals send teams to flooded hospitals and key sites, and to residents who cannot reach a shelter in time. Teams drive on roads that are passable at the moment shown in the video: pause at "water rising" or at the peak, then dispatch.

        Team assumptions

        Priority: hospitals and clinics first, then fire, police and town halls in the water, then groups of residents (more people and earlier waves first). Team numbers, speed and time on scene are assumptions: replace them with the real figures of the emergency zone.

        Be ready before the water comes

          Model vs the real 2021 flood

          Read with care

          • Flood-size maps are model results, not forecasts of a specific day. Check resolution and flood type in Annex 2.
          • Satellite maps miss water under trees, in narrow streets, and water that drained before the satellite passed.
          • "People" means residents: where people live, not where they are during the day.
          • A building counts when its centre is in a flooded cell. It says nothing about damage.
          • The video shows the order in which water arrives, not real hours. Each section fills from its riverbed (water level flat across the valley) and the wave moves downstream using Copernicus DEM ground heights. It is not a hydraulic model: no flow speeds, no blocked bridges, no dam effects. The ripples run downhill; their speed is visual only.
          • Streets, rivers, trees and buildings come from OpenStreetMap. Cars are an illustration, not traffic data.
          • Evacuation routes, waves and "in time" use your assumptions (capacity, walking speed, warning time) and the flood maps. Check them with local services.

          Load your team's data

          Your data person makes three GeoJSON files with the Colab script. All coordinates in EPSG:4326 (longitude, latitude). Only the grid file is required.

          Exact format the app expects 
          { "type": "FeatureCollection",
  "features": [
    { "type": "Feature",
      "geometry": { "type": "Polygon", "coordinates": [[[5.80,50.57],[5.8014,50.57],[5.8014,50.5709],[5.80,50.5709],[5.80,50.57]]] },
      "properties": { "depth_rp10": 0.0, "depth_rp100": 0.8, "depth_rp500": 1.9, "pop": 42 } }
  ] }
• depth_rpN = water depth in metres for the 1-in-N-year flood (0 or null = dry)
• any number of depth_rpN columns; the app finds them by name
• pop = residents in the cell (optional)
• keep each file under ~30 MB: clip to your study area
